## Water in the World

One source — a three-panel figure of where the world's water is — answered eight
times, from Foundation to Year 10, with the evidence-bearing phrases marked and
explained.

### The four criteria

| | Criterion | Marked with |
|---|---|---|
| ■ | Data, origin &amp; purpose | solid underline |
| ▲ | Place, space &amp; scale | dashed underline |
| ● | Interconnection &amp; explanation | dotted underline |
| ◆ | Usefulness &amp; limitations | double underline |

Each criterion carries a colour **and** an underline pattern. Colour alone does
not survive a black-and-white photocopy and is not reliable for colour-blind
students, so the pattern carries the coding when the colour is gone.

## Curriculum

Checked against the **Victorian Curriculum 2.0 Geography Foundation to Level 10**
scope and sequence. Every rung carries its real `VC2HG…` codes.

Two things are worth knowing about how they attach.

**Geography's skills strand is not four parallel ladders.** History's *Using
historical sources* sub-strand has four descriptors that run the length of the
curriculum, so a four-row wall maps onto it cleanly. Geography's sub-strands are
inquiry (S01), collecting (S02), representing (S03), interpreting (S04),
concluding and decision-making (S05–S06) and communicating (S07). The four rows
of this wall are a **teaching structure** that draws across S02 to S05. `VC` in
`water/content.js` records exactly which descriptor each rung claims. Do not read
the rows as sub-strands.

**Interpreting and concluding do not exist below Levels 5 and 6.** S04 and S05
start there. The Foundation to Level 4 rungs therefore claim fewer descriptors —
that is the curriculum, not an omission here.

### What the unit sits on

`VC2HG8K01` water as a renewable resource and the forms it takes in the water
cycle · `VC2HG8K03` the distribution and variability of Australia's water
resources · `VC2HG8K04` the nature of water scarcity and ways of overcoming it.

**A gap worth naming:** K03 is specifically about *Australia's* water resources,
and this source is global. The wall covers the scarcity and distribution half of
the unit and not the Australian half. A second source is needed for that.

## The figure is a classroom redraw

The percentages are the U.S.
Geological Survey's summary of global water storage, after Shiklomanov (1993);
USGS material is in the public domain. The *drawing* is ours, generated by
`water/make-figure.mjs`, and the wall says so out loud — "who made this display,
and why" is half the source analysis.

The fourth slice of panel 3 is a remainder and is labelled as one. Do not
replace it with a list of small stores unless you have a citation for each.

## One file holds the words

`water/content.js` is the only place any wording lives. The page reads from it,
and so does the standalone builder, so the two cannot drift apart.

To change the wall, edit `water/content.js`. To change the figure, edit the
numbers at the top of `water/make-figure.mjs`, then:

```
npm i playwright                          # once
node water/make-figure.mjs                # redraw the figure
python3 tools/build_standalone.py water/bump-it-up.html
```
